# Nex Playground: Copy Cat Deluxe (match the pose, 1 or 2 players)

**Why captured:** closest shipped game to "copy a shape with your body". Shows how a camera game frames the live feed so it is a prop in a game, not a debug feed.

**What to steal:**
- The camera is never full bleed. Each player's feed sits in a tilted white polaroid frame with a thick dark outline and a drop shadow, on a flat mustard background with diagonal stripes (nex-copy-cat-deluxe-a.jpg). The raw room stays visible, but the frame makes it a "photo" and instantly reads as a game. Jester: put the mirrored webcam in a rounded, outlined, slightly rotated frame instead of a black canvas.
- Score lives on a chunky coloured plate hanging off the top of each player's frame (red left, blue right) with big cream numerals, plus a small tab in the middle for "ROUND 5/5". Player colour is the identity, used for plate, frame accent and result row.
- The target sits in the middle on a wooden sign held up by a cartoon paw (nex-copy-cat-deluxe-b.jpg). The thing to copy is a character illustration, not a video; it is small and between the two players, not a strip across the top.
- The result screen is a purple card: SUMMARY, then rows of "24 / SCORE / 33", "ROUNDS WON", "PERFECT POSES", coins that fly in, and mascots dancing in the corners, with the two players' photos tilted either side (nex-copy-cat-deluxe-c.jpg). Row-per-stat with the label centred and each player's number outside is an easy layout to build.
- Type is a heavy rounded display face in cream with a darker outline; everything reads at TV distance.

**What to skip:** the wooden sign and mascot art (needs illustration), and the two-side-by-side layout for live play; Jester passes one laptop so it needs one big frame per turn. The pale-yellow kid palette is too young; keep the structure, change the palette.
